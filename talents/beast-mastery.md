# Beast Mastery
{: .no_toc }

## On this page
{: .no_toc }

1. TOC
{:toc}

As per its name this talent tree focus mostly on improving the animal companion, your pet. Let’s first have a look at a pet’s base stats. Every pet has certain modifiers depending on the family it belongs to, but let’s start with a “neutral” pet, independent of pet family. Whatever family you chose, it will change the numbers – but no drastically in the big picture.

| Stat | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Health | 3052 HP |
| Armor | 4014 |
| Armor value physical damage reduction against lvl 60 enemy | 42,2% |
| Base white DPS | 51.25 DPS |
| Base crit chance | 5 % |
| Focus regeneration | 5 Focus/sec |

A few additional remarks: Pet attack speed is normalized. Meaning that a faster pet does not more damage than a slower one. Broken Tooth with attack speed of 1 attack/sec hits for the listed 51 damage (on average), while a slower pet with a 2 attack/sec hits for 102 damage with each hit. Your pet possess base stats like players have, like stamina, strength, agility and so on. And as I understand it, they work like on a warrior. Meaning an additional point in strength gives 2 attack power, 20 agility give 1% crit chance, 10 stamina give 100 HP and so on.

Talents in Beast Mastery increase your pet’s offense, defensive, movement speed, focus regeneration and give it additional utility in form of a stun, a powerful dps cooldown and immunity to cc. There are also a few talents which affect the Hunter directly. All in all, Beast Mastery talents can greatly increase your pet’s damage and therefore meaningfully add to your own damage output, even when the Hunter has aquired the highest level of gear. While solo adventuring or in dungeons it can add to your defense, because it can soak up damage with its ability to aggro and tank mobs. But in a raid or pvp setting, it does not meaningfully add to your defensive capabilities and serves mainly as a damage increase.

## Put talent points into Beast Mastery if

- You want to increase your and your animal companion’s damage output

- You want to increase your pet’s survivability so that it can tank enemies in the open world and dungeons and better survive in raids

- You want specific utility like improved aspects (Aspect of the Cheetah, Aspect of the Monkey, Aspect of the Hawk) or a 3s stun that is delivered by your pet (Intimidation)

Let’s evaluate all the Beast Mastery talents.

## Talents

### [Improved Aspect of the Hawk](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19556)

**What it changes:** At 5/5 it gives your ranged attacks a 5% change to activate Quick Shot for 10s, which increases the speed you can shot your ranged abilities by 30%. Affects the rate at which you shot Auto Attacks and also affects the casting time of Aimed Shot and Multi-Shot.

**My assessment:** A-Tier. The attack speed increase is always 30%, just the change to proc is increased by 1% per point. Always a good choice and especially strong in situations you can freely shot at your target, like on boss fights in raids.

### [Endurance Training](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19587)

**What it changes:** Increases your pets health by 3% per point up to 15% at 5/5.

**My assessment:** B-Tier. Beast Mastery has a few talents to increase your pets survivability. Endurance Training is generally the most useful of them. It increases your pets overall health pool which helps against all types of damage and also against burst damage.

### [Improved Eyes of the Beast](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19558)

**What it changes:** At 2/2 Increases the duration of Eyes of the Beast by 1 minute.

**My assessment:** D-Tier. Very niche and I consider it a “fun” talent. Can be useful for scouting with your pet, but Eagle Eye is better.

### [Improved Aspect of the Monkey](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=24387)

**What it changes:** Increases Aspect of the Monkey’s additional dodge chance by 1% per point up to 5%.

**My assessment:** C-Tier. Niche and I would only consider it in very specific pvp scenarios when I need more survivability against melee. But the points can be spent better in the Survival tree for that purpose.

### [Thick Hide](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19612)

**What it changes:** Increases your pets armor by 10% per point up to 30% at 3/3.

**My assessment:** B-Tier. The second talent to address pet survivability. Reduces melee damage by about 10% at 3/3. I would chose this over Endurance Training if I know the pet will receive mostly physical damage.

### [Improved Revive Pet](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19575)

**What it changes:** Decreases cast time of Revive Pet significantly (4s instead of 10s), reduces mana cost and increases the amount of health your pet has after reviving.

**My assessment:** A-Tier. Without this talent, it’s impractical to revive your pet during a fight. This talent changes that and is well worth the points, when your game plan relies on your pet being alive. Also useful if you need to pull with your pet in a raid.

### [Pathfinding](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19560)

**What it changes:** Increases Aspect of the Cheetah and Packs movement speed increase by 3% per point up to 6% at 2/2.

**My assessment:** B-Tier. Strong while leveling, in dungeons (e.g. DMT farming), while in the open world and in pvp. Not a necessary talent but always nice to have.

### [Bestial Swiftness](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19596)

**What it changes:** Increases your pets outdoor movement speed by 30% at 1/1.

**My assessment:** B-Tier. Only works outdoor. Indirectly increases your pet’s damage output and utility since it can connect faster and more consistently on an enemy. I would consider this when I rely on my pet for damage and know I will be outdoors a lot like in the open world, battle grounds and even some raids and dungeons.

### [Unleashed Fury](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19620)

**What it changes:** At 5/5 provides a 20% damage increase to your pet.

**My assessment:** A-Tier. The strongest passive damage increase from talents for your pet. Includes white damage (auto attacks) and yellow damage (from pet abilities).

### [Improved Mend Pet](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19573)

**What it changes:** Mend Pet is channeled for 5s at the talent adds the chance to remove a Curse, Disease, Magic or Poison effect from your pet. At 1/2 a 15% every second and at 2/2 at 50% chance.

**My assessment:** B-Tier. Niche but strong. While leveling and solo adventuring I would consider investing 1/2 into this talent to be able to remove some long-lasting diseases and curses. At 2/2 Mend Pet becomes a strong utility spell which reliably frees your pet from almost all negative effects.

### [Ferocity](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19602)

**What it changes:** At 5/5 it increases your pets chance to critically hit by 15%.

**My assessment:** A-Tier. Your pet has a base crit chance of 5%. Ferocity increases it significantly to 20%. After Unleashed Fury the second strongest passive damage increase from talents.

### [Spirit Bond](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=20895)

**What it changes:** At 2/2 every 10s the pet and its master will regenerate 2% of their maximum health.

**My assessment:** B-Tier. Spirit Bond is very different from Endurance Training and Thick Hide: it gives sustain, not immediate effective HP. A “neutral” pet generates 61 HP every 10s or around 360 HP/min. It does not counter bursts of damage like the other two talents, but becomes valuable in prolonged engagements, like in longer duels, while farming etc. The scales with your and your pet’s health. Niche, but valuable given the right circumstances. The pet needs to be active for the talent to work.

### [Intimidation](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19577)

**What it changes:** Command your pet to stun the target for 3s.

**My assessment:** S-Tier. One of our strongest Ccs and our only stun. It doesn’t share diminishing returns with Freezing Trap. The skill is activated instantly by the Hunter and puts a 15s buff on the pet. The stun is delivered with your pet’s next attack. The talent is always worth taking when spending more than 20 points into Beast Mastery as it is strong in itself and useful in all environments. It needs some awareness and coordination with your pet.

### [Bestial Discipline](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19592)

**What it changes:** Increases your pets focus regeneration by 20% at 2/2.

**My assessment:** C-Tier. The base focus regeneration of 5 focus/s is increased to 6 focus/s. This seems like negligible, but can mean that your pet gets use an extra ability or two during a boss fight. Going this deep into Beast Mastery means that you rely on your pet for damage and it helps with that. The drawback is, that there might be better talents to spend your points on. Frenzy, Ferocity and Unleashed Fury are stronger dps increase. Use it in a pet focused build in which you don’t need the utility of Improved Mend Pet, the sustain of Spirit Bond or improved aspects.

### [Frenzy](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19625)

**What it changes:** At 5/5 your pet has a 100% to gain the Frenzy buff which increases your pet’s attack speed by 30% for 10s.

**My assessment:** A-Tier. Your third passive dps increase for your pet. It only becomes available when going deep into Beast Mastery. It translates into a dps increase of roughly 24%. It favors a pet with fast attack speed, since that increases uptime. With a pet like Broken Tooth spending 4/5 can be an option and frees up one talent point.

### [Bestial Wrath](https://www.wowhead.com/classic/spell=19574)

**What it changes:** Send your pet into a rage, causing 50% additional damage for 18s. During this time your pet is immune to Cc effects.

**My assessment:** S-Tier. The capstone talent in Beast Mastery and our strongest dps cooldown. The pet will enlarge and glow red, so the enemies will be aware when it’s active. It’s useful to drastically increase your pets damage output and make it stick to the target. I rate this ability highly even tough it’s rarely used at endgame. The reason being, that pets do not scale with our gear and their damage contribution diminishes over time.
