# Talents in-depth
{: .no_toc }

## How to evaluate a talent

Talent choices shape how a class plays. From level 10 onward, you earn one talent point per level, giving you 51 points to spend at level 60. You can use these points to improve existing abilities or unlock new ones. Some talents require you to invest further in a tree before they become available. For example, Aimed Shot becomes available after you spend 10 points in Marksmanship. The 11th, 21st, and 31st points in a tree often unlock abilities that can define a build.

Hunters specialize in ranged damage. Our talent trees preserve that core playstyle while strengthening different parts of our toolkit: our pet, our ranged attacks, or our ability to survive and control a fight. Experimenting with talents is one of the best ways to discover what the class can do.

Many guides recommend a small selection of builds. These are useful starting points, but no single build is best at everything. The right choices depend on what you are doing, the group you are playing with, and the tools you value most. Use the builds in this handbook as starting points, then adapt them to your circumstances and playstyle.

## How I rate talents

For a bit of fun, I have also ranked every talent from S to D. These ratings reflect my preferences and the situations discussed below; they are not a universal ranking for every activity.

- **S-tier:** Build-defining talents. I build around them or consider them essential to the playstyle I am pursuing.

- **A-tier:** Strong talents that I usually take when I invest far enough in the tree to reach them.

- **B-tier:** Valuable options that compete with other worthwhile choices. Whether I take them depends on the build.

- **C-tier:** Niche talents, or talents whose benefits are usually too limited to justify the points.

- **D-tier:** Talents I would not take; I consider the alternatives a better use of the points.
