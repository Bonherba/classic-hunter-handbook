# Survival
{: .no_toc }

<figure class="hunter-art hunter-portrait">
<img src="{{ '/assets/images/talents/survival.png' | relative_url }}" alt="A Hunter crouching in a forest with a blade" width="518" height="568">
</figure>

This talent tree focuses on improving utility and survivability, but also increases our damage output in various ways. Survival does what the name suggests – it bolsters our defense and durability, especially against melee opponents and complements MM and BM by addressing our weakness in melee. It offers passive bonuses as well as active abilities to aid our survival. Throughout the tree we also find talents to increase our damage output and when we invest enough points into it, we can gain access to talents powerful enough to rival MM’s damage increases. However, we don’t gain a party-wide damage increase like Trueshot Aura and therefore deep Survival builds are more suited for solo adventuring, farming, dungeons or PvP instead of high-end raiding, since we should never put our own damage output above a group-wide increase that benefits the whole squad.

I really like Survival because it has lots of strong and competing talents (and no useless ones) which give us the opportunity to make the appropriate choices for the specific situation we want to tackle.

## Put points into Survival

- For situational damage increases against certain types of enemies, when melee weaving, or when including traps in our rotation. Additionally, Lightning Reflexes enables a well-rounded and versatile build that aims at maximizing attack power and crit chance by stacking Agility, while picking up all the defensive and CC benefits included in Survival.

- To increase survivability, especially against melee aggressors, by gaining active abilities and passive bonuses to mitigate and soak damage or avoid it altogether by slowing and rooting enemies.

- To gain access to Entrapment which transforms Frost Trap into a powerful zoning tool to hinder enemies in choke points/areas which is generally strong, but especially in PvP.

During its duration, melee attackers will have a hard time dealing damage to you, while you get parry-hasted (your next swing occurs earlier when you parry an attack). An enemy of the same level has a 4% chance to resist your traps. 1 point into Trap Mastery reduces this to 1%. It can never fall below 1%. I often put a point into it while in PvP to make my traps hit as reliably as possible. With 0 points, enemies of a higher level have a chance of 5% (level 61), 6% (level 62) and 17% (level 63, skull level) to resist. So put as many points into it as needed for your use case. |

The second effect becomes useful in PvP because it provides an additional chance to resist slowing and immobilizing effects from abilities such as Frost Nova, Frostbolt, and Hamstring. 15% doesn’t sound like much, but resisting those effects can mean victory or death and it will add up over time. |
